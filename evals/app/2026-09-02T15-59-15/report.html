Findings, ranked

  1. blocker

    Blob is full, and a full Blob leaves episodes stuck forever.

    Vercel Blob (Hobby, 1 GB) holds 1007.9 MB. From about ten minutes into the run every settle failed with “Storage quota exceeded”. 6 episodes rendered fine at fal and are stuck in “generating”; the viewer sees “Filming… a few minutes” with no end and no error. There is no failed state for a settle error, only for a render error.

    Fix Treat a settle error as a failure after a few attempts, or add a “rendered, not stored” state. Separately, free space: the six eval series hold about 180 MB; older series hold the rest (see appendix).

  2. high

    A failing settle re-runs in full on every poll, from every open tab.

    Each 5-second poll on a stuck episode downloads the clip again (about 12 MB), calls the choice writer again, and retries both uploads. Measured in about 15 minutes: 247 failed settle attempts across 6 episodes, 269 choice-writer calls for 16 episodes, 3.4 GB pushed at Blob.

    Fix A settling lock or status, and backoff. The choices call should not run until the clip is stored.

  3. high

    Even a healthy settle is longer than the poll interval, so one tab settles the same episode twice.

    storeClip median is 5.6 s against a 5 s poll. The first series' opening was settled twice by the single browser tab, five seconds apart, before my poller existed (server log 00:10:41 and 00:10:46). Across the run 7 of 10 healthy episodes settled 2 to 4 times, though those counts include my own poller.

    Fix Same fix as above: mark the row settling, or have the poll await the in-flight settle.

  4. high

    A non-human protagonist breaks point of view at the render.

    The cat series: all three clips show an orange cat from behind, a follow camera rather than the cat's eyes. By depth two the camera had migrated to the human who walked in. The writer's first sentence was right (“first-person POV of a house cat, low to the floor”, paws from the bottom of frame); the model read “paws creeping forward” as a visible cat.

    Fix Iterate the non-human first sentence on the test bench: bun run root "i'm a house cat in a tokyo apartment" --render. Until it holds, the premise rule for animals is a promise the render does not keep.

  5. medium

    The wait copy says “a few minutes”; the wait is 10 to 16 seconds.

    Submit to settled, healthy episodes: median 12 s (fal reports about 4.3 s of inference; the rest is the poll and the settle). Tap to play is about 15 to 20 s once the next poll lands.

    Fix Say “a few seconds”, or nothing.

  6. medium

    One child in ten never shows its move, and its last beat rendered as a deformity.

    Raccoon, “Slam the cooler door shut”: no hand and no cooler door in any sampled frame; the cooler stays closed. The prompt's “turns its head completely upside down between its front paws” became a second face.

    Fix Nothing structural; it is the model's miss rate on a physical move. Worth tracking as a number over more runs.

  7. medium

    The expander stacks a second cliffhanger on premises that already have one, and drifts surreal on plain ones.

    Bus stop and Sopranos: all six scenes keep the premise's cliffhanger and add another (sedans, a social security number read aloud, a man under a bus; feds, a house fire, a caller). The Sopranos scenes also copied the premise's first person (“to me”, “my knuckles”). Bakery: all three scenes went supernatural for “a bakery at dawn”.

    Fix One rule to consider: if the premise ends on a cliffhanger, the scene ends on that one.

  8. low

    Two writer format slips in five child prompts.

    Cat, depth two: dialogue tagged [Japanese] but written in English. Bus stop child: a voice from the phone speaks with no speaker tag, because only visible characters may speak. The rule has no answer for a voice on a phone, which is a legitimate off-screen speaker.

    Fix Decide whether off-screen voices exist; if yes, give them a tag rule.

  9. low

    Choice pairs: 4 of 9 are two physical acts of the same kind.

    Laundromat root (hit the stop / pry the handle), cat root (swat / snag), cat child (tip / bite), bakery root (swat / catch). Every label is verb-first and 3 to 5 words. One pair points at a sound-only beat the viewer may not have registered.

    Fix The pair rule holds a little over half the time. The eval flags will not catch “same kind”; it needs a reader.

  10. low

    Titles are literal in all six, and wrong in two.

    “Elderly Woman Drops Phone”: she hands it over and runs. “Pigeon Escapes the Balcony Rail”: it only starts to spread its wings.

    Fix The writer sees the prompt, which ends before the outcome; it is inferring an ending. “Names what happens” could be “names what is shown”.

  11. unverified

    Enter in the typed-move field did not submit through my browser tool.

    Typing a move and pressing Enter left the value in the field twice in a row; submitting the form programmatically worked and the branch was created. This is likely my key tool, not the app, but I could not prove it.

    Fix Press Enter in the field by hand once.

  12. good

    What held.

Point of view held in 13 of 16 clips, every miss being the cat. Spoken moves were handled right both times: the child opens on the answer and the protagonist's words are never shown. How to read my verdicts

  • Frames, not motion. Six frames at 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 99 percent, the same for every clip. A beat between samples can be missed; a beat I call missing may sit between frames. The strips are under each clip so you can check me.
  • No audio. Dialogue delivery, the soundscape, and whether the protagonist's voice leaks in are unjudged. The bus stop child and both spoken-move children are the ones to listen to.
  • Seam is a mean. The number is a mean absolute gray difference on a 96×170 downsample, so a small local change (a phone flipping open) would not move it. I checked three pairs by eye; they matched.
  • My poller doubled the polling on healthy episodes, so the “settled 2 to 4 times” count overstates a single viewer. The single-tab double settle on the first opening happened before my poller started.
  • One rater, sixteen clips. Pass, weak, fail; no scores.
  • Five clips were never settled because of the Blob quota. I pulled them from fal's result by request id (read-only) so they could be judged; their written moves do not exist.
